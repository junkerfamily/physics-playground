Water can climb out of a tank.

Fill the tube once and water keeps flowing up and over the top with no pump. It runs as long as the far end sits in lower water. The drawing is to scale, so you can test the real height limit.

What drives it

Gravity supplies the energy. The water going down the long side falls farther than the water rising on the short side, so the whole column keeps moving toward the lower surface.

What lifts the water

Air presses on the tank surface while pressure inside the rising tube drops. That difference pushes water up to the peak. The pink dots show where the pressure is lowest.

Why there is a limit

Air pressure can hold up only about 10 m of water. Past that, or sooner when flowing fast, the water boils into a vapor gap at the peak. This model simplifies pipe friction.
