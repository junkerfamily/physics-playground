One turn, one bang.

A two-stroke engine fires on every turn of the crank. It manages this by using both sides of the piston at once. The top squeezes and burns fuel while the bottom pumps in the next batch.

Why it's simple

The piston itself opens and closes the ports as it slides past them. There are no cams or valves in the head, which keeps chainsaws, dirt bikes, and outboard motors light and cheap.

Why it's powerful

A four-stroke engine fires every other turn. A two-stroke fires every turn, so a small one can make a lot of power for its weight.

Why it's dirty

The exhaust and transfer ports are open together, so some fresh fuel blows straight out the exhaust. Oil is mixed into the fuel to lubricate the crank, and it burns too. That's the blue smoke.
