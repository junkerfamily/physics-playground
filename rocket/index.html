A rocket pushes on itself.

Burning fuel makes hot gas that slams into every wall of the engine. The front wall gets hit, but the back is open, so nothing balances that push. The gas escapes backward and the rocket goes forward, even in empty space.

No air needed

A rocket doesn't push against the air or the ground. The gas pushes on the inside of the engine. That's why rockets work best in space, where nothing gets in the way.

Momentum evens out

Every bit of gas thrown backward gives the rocket an equal kick forward. Faster exhaust gives a bigger kick from the same amount of fuel.

Why rockets are mostly fuel

Reaching orbit takes about 9.4 km/s, including losses to gravity and air. Even a rocket that's 90% fuel falls short with 3 km/s exhaust, which is why big rockets drop empty stages along the way.
