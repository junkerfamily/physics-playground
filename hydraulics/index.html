Push a little. Lift a lot.

This is a hydraulic jack cut open. Each dot is a parcel of oil. Oil barely compresses, so pressure from your small push reaches every surface at once, including a much larger piston under the load.

Why force multiplies

Pressure is force spread over area. The same pressure pressing on 9 times the area makes 9 times the force.

What you pay for it

The oil you push out has to fill a much wider cylinder, so the load rises only 1/9 as far as your hand moves. Force goes up, distance goes down, and the work stays equal.

Why it holds still

A one-way valve lets oil into the big cylinder and blocks it from coming back. Between strokes, the trapped oil holds the load. This model ignores friction and leaks.
